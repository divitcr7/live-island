Live Island · open source · design study 01

An island that stays.

Every Android island so far copies a pill that pops up, says one thing and disappears. Live Island stays on screen around your camera, runs several activities at once, shows progress on its own edge, and responds to touch. It doesn't borrow Material, iOS or One UI styling. The look is black glass, a lit rim and monospaced numbers.

  • Never vanishes. At rest it shows your next meeting, battery and weather around the lens.
  • The rim is the readout. Song position, countdowns and trip progress run around its edge.
  • Many at once. Extra activities split off as satellites that melt back in.
  • Touch it. Swipe between activities, hold for commands, drop files on it.

The rethink

Six rules the island follows.

Each rule fixes a specific complaint about the islands that exist today: they hide when idle, show one thing at a time, mirror notifications without adding anything, and don't know where your camera is.

Presence

It never leaves

Idle means ambient, not gone. The smallest state is a lit ring around the lens, and you choose how small it gets.

Rim

The edge carries the number

Progress, countdowns, trip distance and charge run around the perimeter, so you can read them out of the corner of your eye without opening anything.

Satellites

More than one thing

A timer and music at the same time? The second activity splits into a satellite bubble and merges back when the first one ends.

Hands

Gestures, not just taps

Swipe sideways to switch activities, down to open, up to shrink. Hold for a command bar. Double-tap runs the main action.

Shelf

It holds things

Drag a photo, link or text onto it. It keeps them in a shelf until you drop them into another app.

Lens

It knows where the camera is

It reads the display cutout and anchors there, whether the camera is centred, in a corner, or under the screen.

Contact sheet

Every state, live.

These are all running: the timer counts down, the song plays, and the rims fill in real time. The finish you pick above applies here too.

Gesture map

Eight gestures, one surface.

You can try the first five on the phone at the top of the page with a mouse or a finger.

Cutout aware

Wherever the lens is, it grows from there.

With a corner camera, a centred pill sits away from the lens and looks out of place. Live Island reads DisplayCutout.boundingRects and grows out of the hole instead.

Finishes

Four finishes, no borrowed design language.

The body stays black so it blends into the camera hole on OLED. Only the rim changes. Pick one to restyle the whole page.

How it gets built

Android APIs behind each piece.

PieceAndroid API
Always-on overlayAccessibilityService window of type TYPE_ACCESSIBILITY_OVERLAY, which can draw over the status bar, unlike SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW.
Music + rim progressMediaSessionManager.getActiveSessions() using the notification listener as the token. Position comes from PlaybackState, controls from TransportControls.
Notifications, timers, ridesNotificationListenerService. Read progress extras and Android 16 ProgressStyle live updates.
Lens anchoringWindowInsets.getDisplayCutout() → boundingRects, recalculated on rotation.
Satellite meltCompose Canvas + AGSL RuntimeShader metaballs (Android 13+), with a blur and alpha-threshold fallback.
MotionJetpack Compose spring() on width, height and corner radius. Content crossfades with blur.
Camera / mic watchCameraManager.AvailabilityCallback + AudioManager.registerAudioRecordingCallback.
ChargingSticky ACTION_BATTERY_CHANGED + BatteryManager.computeChargeTimeRemaining().
Shelf dropsOverlay OnDragListener for global drags (only when the source app allows them), plus a share-sheet target as a fallback.
What already exists

Existing apps

  • dynamicSpotThe most popular one. Closed source and freemium; lock-screen support is paid.
  • LiveBridgeOpen source, written in Dart. Builds on the system pill.
  • IslandifyKotlin and Compose with Material 3 styling. Notifications, music, calls, charging.
  • Android-Dynamic-IslandApache-2.0. A customisable starting point.
  • NothingLand, Material You IslandEach one adopts another brand's visual style.
What they all lack: the island hides when idle, shows one activity at a time, only mirrors notifications, uses another brand's look, and reviewers note it looks wrong with a corner camera. Live Island is designed around fixing those five problems.
